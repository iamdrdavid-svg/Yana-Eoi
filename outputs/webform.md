**8.3 Why the Housing SEPP in-fill affordable housing bonus is not used**
The in-fill bonus allows up to 30% extra height where 15% of floor space is affordable for 15 years. On this site that lifts the 16 m height limit to about 20.8 m, roughly six storeys. That does not meet the clause 4.1A minimum site area. It would deliver a small fraction of the 243 homes. A council planning proposal is open in law, but council's precinct planning is still being updated, and the HDA pathway lets the State, council, agencies and the community test the controls, design and public benefit together.
> Source notes: R-f2 (LOCATED). Pack p6. Urbis verification of bonus arithmetic pending (pack p6: "subject to Urbis verification").

**8.4 Strategic merit**
- **Housing supply where the State has invested.** The site is about 623 m walk from Castle Hill Metro, in an established centre with retail, services and jobs. The Hills Shire has a target of 23,300 completed homes by 2029. `[verify R-g1]`
- **Realisable, not nominal, capacity.** The State Land Use Plan's feasible housing capacity policy separates zoned capacity from capacity that will actually be built. This site is consolidated, serviced, with a defined scheme and programme, so its capacity is realisable within the Accord period. `[verify R-e2 wording]`
- **Consistent with the centre's emerging scale.** The HDA has supported towers of up to 40 storeys in Castle Hill, e.g. 16-20 Old Castle Hill Road (371 homes). `[verify R-c1 status]`
- **Neighbours remain developable.** The concept shows indicative footprints for 81-87 Cecil Avenue (8,416 m²) and the adjoining lot to the south-west (2,600 m²), with 22-25 m street separation.
> Source notes: A8, B16, R-c1, R-e2, R-g1. Pack p5, p16, p23. Hard rule 2: no reliance on Sydney Plan Appendix D map, no radius claim, s9.1 not asserted as binding.
