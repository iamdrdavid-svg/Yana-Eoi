**Q48. Summary of the rezoning proposal and its strategic merit**
The rezoning amends the height of buildings map and the minimum lot size standard for one consolidated R4 site, to deliver 222 to 243 homes, including affordable homes in perpetuity, an eight-minute walk from Castle Hill Metro.

Strategic merit:
- **Homes where the infrastructure already is.** The site sits in the walking catchment of a Metro station and a centre of jobs, shops, health and education. This is consistent with The Sydney Plan's direction to increase housing supply in well-connected locations, and with The Hills' target of 23,300 homes by 2029.
- **A site able to accommodate height.** Taller buildings belong where access to services and the ability to accommodate growth support them. This site is a corner holding in single ownership, with two street frontages and no amalgamation required. It is already in the consolidated state that the Castle Hill Precinct Plan has to seek elsewhere through land assembly.
- **Capacity that will be built.** The Feasible Housing Capacity Policy separates zoned capacity from capacity that is realisable. The Precinct Plan's own yield projections assume much of this area will not develop because of fragmented ownership. This site is the exception: it is consolidated, serviced, and has a defined scheme.
- **Scale in its emerging context.** Castle Hill's high-density area is already changing through State-assessed housing. That includes 244–248 Old Northern Road on the same block (up to 200 apartments, SSD-106965706), 93–107 Cecil Avenue and 9–10 Roger Avenue on the same street at a similar distance from the Metro (610 apartments in 23 to 35 storey buildings, SSD-78156221), and 16–20 Old Castle Hill Road (40 storeys, SSD-85238209). The concept is tested between 35 and 38 storeys against this context, not against the 2024 controls it would replace, which predate The Sydney Plan.
- **No other State-assessed pathway.** See Q50.
> Source notes: R-h4, R-k1, R-k5, R-k6, R-j5, R-j6; Castle Hill Precinct Plan 2024 Table 7 (Area F uptake 55%/60%). Language note: "well-connected locations" and the site-capability test (access to services, ability to accommodate growth) are DPHI's justification terms. **Red-team note: the two June 2026 Cecil Avenue refusals (24 and 25 storeys, "out of context", "about 12 storeys") and the September LMR refusals remain the decisive risk at this height. **Q50. Details of changes, including whether additional to existing bonuses**
The changes are sought instead of, not on top of, the in-fill affordable housing bonus. That bonus adds 30% to the height and floor space otherwise available. On this site, the base is the Low and Mid-Rise housing standard of `[planner to confirm: 17.5 m and 1.5:1 (outer area) or 22 m and 2.2:1 (inner area)]`. With the bonus, that gives 22.75 m and 1.95:1, about 34 homes, or at most 28.6 m and 2.86:1, about 49 homes. Either result is below the 100-home threshold for State significance, and well short of what this consolidated site next to a Metro station can sustain. On Cecil Avenue, the only other route to this scale is a planning proposal followed by a bonus application, as at 93–107 Cecil Avenue, which takes two steps and several years. A concurrent rezoning secures the same outcome in a single assessment. No other State-assessed pathway can deliver this housing on this site.
